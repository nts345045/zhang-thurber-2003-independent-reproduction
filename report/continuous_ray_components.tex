\subsection{Continuous layered-ray components}

The finite-polygon endpoint discrepancy motivates a separate continuous-curve
calculation. This component retains the original isotropic Fermat functional
and the finite model box. Straight segments represent constant-speed and
vertical legs; circular arcs represent positive affine depth gradients; a
horizontal guide may lie on the physical bottom. The curve used for integration
is distinct from its sampled display polygon. Its branch information does not
set a polygon gradient or production convergence flag.

For a single affine interval, $v(z)=v_0+g(z-z_0)$ with $g>0$, the unbounded
arc travel time is
\begin{equation}
 T=\frac{2}{g}\operatorname{arsinh}
 \left(\frac{gD}{2\sqrt{v_s v_r}}\right),
\end{equation}
where $D$ is the Euclidean endpoint separation and $v_s,v_r$ are the endpoint
speeds. A finite-bottom guide replaces an inadmissibly deep arc when horizontal
range reaches
\begin{equation}
 R_* = \frac{\sqrt{v_b^2-v_s^2}+\sqrt{v_b^2-v_r^2}}{g},
\end{equation}
where $v_b$ is the bottom speed. The implementation includes the homogeneous
and vertical limits. It stores a small angular sweep independently of the
starting angle to avoid cancellation in nearly straight arcs. It does not yet
enumerate general piecewise-layered or three-dimensional branches.

The integration component retains every nodal trilinear basis function $N_j$,
including the lateral model degrees of freedom about an exactly depth-only
base. On each supplied curve it calculates
\begin{equation}
 T=\int\frac{ds}{v},\qquad
 \frac{\partial T}{\partial v_j}=-\int\frac{N_j}{v^2}\,ds,\qquad
 \ell_j=\int N_j\,ds.
\end{equation}
The final quantity supplies the existing DD length mask. Each curve piece is
split at all crossed $x$, $y$, and $z$ grid planes. Sixteen-point quadrature
supplies the result; comparison with eight-point quadrature is a numerical
diagnostic, not an interval error bound. The global depth-only check uses
exact nodal equality. A lateral model change therefore invalidates this
specialization. Unique nodal envelope derivatives also require a unique
relevant ray; supplied tied or incomplete branch status remains unresolved.

Two independent sets of invented controls passed. Across nine homogeneous,
affine, reversed, bottom-guided and nearly homogeneous cases, the largest
integrated-time discrepancy from the separate closed-form calculation is
$5.42\times10^{-19}$ s, and receiver closure is within
$1.27\times10^{-18}$ km. Central differences of the analytic travel time agree
with source-momentum components within $2.63\times10^{-13}$ s/km. Separate
polynomial and trigonometric antiderivatives check every nodal kernel in
homogeneous and affine fixtures. Their largest velocity-partial and nodal-length
differences are $3.80\times10^{-19}$ s$^2$/km and
$1.52\times10^{-18}$ km. The complete 58 and 24 checks, sources and replay
commands are preserved in
\path{data/development/layered_curved_components_001/}.
The documented fresh-directory replay rebuilt both control programs and
reproduced all three scientific result files byte for byte.

Two subsequent tests used reference-selected rays from the authoritative
Loma Prieta geometry: a bottom-guided ray and an interior-turning ray.
Construction completed with six and five pieces, respectively. Both calls
to the unchanged integrator failed because a quadrature node rounded to a
partition endpoint. Neither produced a completed native integral or display.
The largest intended-versus-stored depth-join differences were
$8.67\times10^{-19}$ and $1.30\times10^{-18}$ km. These saved discrepancies
are distinct from the reported integration failure; the separate partition
diagnosis below locates the actual failure. The complete inputs, identities, selected
references and failures are retained in
\path{data/development/layered_reference_selected_curves_001/}.

An isolated exception-only diagnostic localized both failures to terminal
partitions near the 3 km depth plane. The bottom-guided case failed on
$[1-2^{-63},1]$ in piece 3, partition 1; the interior-turning case failed on
$[1-2^{-58},1]$ in piece 0, partition 1, using zero-based indices. The
working format has 64 significand bits, with spacing $2^{-64}$ immediately
below one. The failed Gauss nodes lay only 0.47447 and 0.33917 such spacings
above the lower partition endpoint before rounding. Both rounded to that
endpoint, despite positive interval width and a representable midpoint.
The later candidate partitions near zero depth were not reached by these
failed calls. The diagnostic preserves all original result fields except
the enriched exception message and retains identical native identifiers.

A separate implementation evaluates quadrature in a local coordinate
$q\in(0,1)$ for each unchanged stored partition $[a,b]$:
\begin{equation}
 t=a+(b-a)q,\qquad ds=L(b-a)\,dq,
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the curve piece's constant parameter speed. Near a piece's end,
the sample is evaluated using the small backward distance
$(1-b)+(b-a)(1-q)$. The exact straight-line and circular-arc identities
retain the same curve and measure; endpoint and trigonometric rounding
change the working arithmetic. Stored face roots, physical-domain checks
and all nodal contributions remain. No positive partition is dropped,
merged or snapped. Endpoint roundtrip and forward/backward midpoint
discrepancies are observations, not error bounds or promotion criteria.

This version passes the unchanged 58 affine and 24 full-nodal checks.
An additional constant-speed vertical fixture crosses from $z=1\,\mathrm{km}$ to
$z=-\delta$, where $\delta=2^{-63}\,\mathrm{km}$, in a grid with depth nodes
$[-1,0,1]$ km. At fixed lateral coordinates, the exact sum of length-kernel
weights on the lower depth plane is
\begin{equation}
 \int_{-\delta}^{0}\frac{-z}{1\,\mathrm{km}}\,dz
 =\frac{\delta^2}{2\,\mathrm{km}}=2^{-127}\,\mathrm{km}.
\end{equation}
The computed mass differs by one unit in the last place and passes the declared
relative allowance of 512 machine epsilons. Positivity, travel time and
the two-subpiece, 48-sample inventory also pass. This checks a nonzero
microscopic contribution independently of the quadrature implementation;
it does not bound every native nodal kernel. The failure localization,
new method and finite controls are preserved in
\path{data/development/layered_local_quadrature_001/}.

The two original native cases then complete with unchanged inputs and
construction fields: 15 subpieces and 360 samples for the bottom-guided ray,
and eight subpieces and 192 samples for the interior-turning ray. Their
travel times are 13.315127513112486 and 3.6600250681424327 s. The former
exceeds its saved reference interval by about $8.15\times10^{-20}$ s; the
latter differs from its saved scalar reference by $-2.98\times10^{-16}$ s.
The scalar comparison supplies no interval ordering or minimum-time proof.
Eight-versus-sixteen-point action differences are $3.49\times10^{-13}$ and
$1.50\times10^{-9}$ s; these are diagnostics, not error bounds. Both results
retain all 288 nodal length and velocity-kernel entries, with their saved
sum and scaling identities checked. The largest forward/backward midpoint
discrepancies are $6.99\times10^{-18}$ and $8.67\times10^{-19}$ km. Those
finite observations do not certify compatibility at every old face root.
No independent native all-node kernel oracle or optimized-source derivative
assessment accompanies these two completed integrals.

These results establish a limited continuous-curve component. No real-data
inversion, updated-model accuracy result, or manuscript-panel promotion follows
from these tests.
